# Research notes: shock_set_distance

This bundle was built by a metric-building agent from two idea pages, `docs/ideas/shock_geometry/shock_set_distance.md` and `docs/ideas/shock_geometry/shock_detector.md`. Both pages were deleted by the build itself. The work went in as pull request #22 on 2026-10-30. An adversarial review round followed. The work log dates that round 2026-10-31, and the routing record dates it 2026-10-30. This file holds what the builder and the reviewer read, tried and concluded that the card does not carry, condensed from their work log and records. The card is the source of truth for what the metric is, and where the two disagree the card wins. The coding record is in [code_notes.md](code_notes.md).

## Summary

The bundle adds a pressure-only shock detector, `shock_mask`, as shared code for the shock metrics that follow. It also adds a distance between the two detected shock sets, the average symmetric surface distance (ASSD). The detector is the pressure half of Schaal and Springel's (2015) criterion (iii) at Mach 1.3. It was chosen because its threshold is a published physical value; the idea page's normalised gradient sensor has no published threshold. On the `sod_shock_tube` evidence run the metric follows both translation axes and additive noise, as pre-registered. Integer shifts give exactly $(s - 1/2)$ cells, because the three-cell stencil flags two cells at a sharp jump. Every calibrated blur and most spectral filters remove the shock from the detector, so the metric scores the empty-set value (the domain diagonal) there and carries no distance information. The review found that the first convention for non-positive pressure inverted the ranking. It was replaced. The conventions for empty sets and non-positive pressure remain open ([issue 102](../../issues/102-shock-set-distance-empty-and-non-positive-conventions.md)).

## Method choices

- **Detector form.** The builder chose Schaal and Springel's pressure criterion (iii) over the idea page's normalised pressure gradient $\Delta x |\nabla p| / (p + \epsilon)$. The criterion's threshold is a published physical value (Mach 1.3) and needs no tuning. Only the pressure half is applied, because a metric receives one field. Criteria (i) and (ii) need the velocity and the temperature and density fields ([issue 096](../../issues/096-several-fields-handed-to-one-metric.md)).
- **Stencil and axes.** A cell is tested against its two neighbours, following "neighbouring cells" in the source. A face stencil would give $|x - x'|$ exactly for a sharp step, but it sees less of a smeared shock. The jump is tested along each axis and the results are OR-ed, because the shock direction needs the temperature gradient. This was the agent's own reasoning. A consequence is that a sharp step moved $s \ge 1$ cells scores $(s - 1/2)$ cells, not $s$. This was measured on run `shock_set_distance_1793350452`, where `translate_x` gave exactly 0.5, 1.5, 3.5, 7.5 and 15.5 cells at shifts 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16. The builder derived this, tested it and stated it on the card (card, Definition, Equation (3)) rather than forcing the reduction to $|x - x'|$ the brief anticipated.
- **The value of $\gamma$.** The ratio of specific heats is read from the default of the Sod reader, the only compressible dataset with pressure, so that 1.4 has one definition. This was the agent's own reasoning. After the review, the card says this is the reader's default and does not follow an override (see Review round).
- **Registered summary.** The registered summary is the ASSD, because the idea page names it first and Yeghiazaryan and Voiculescu state it plainly. The builder also considered the HD (Taha & Hanbury Eq. 48), the quantile HD (HD95), the AVD (Eq. 50) and MASD. Metrics Reloaded prefers MASD, but its formula could not be read. The four are kept as a proposal ([proposals/MTR-shock_set_distance-other-surface-distance-summaries.md](../../proposals/MTR-shock_set_distance-other-surface-distance-summaries.md)). The first claim, that they all agree for one shock per field, was found false in the review (see Review round).
- **Set points.** Every flagged cell is a point of the set and no boundary is extracted, because a shock is already thin. This was the agent's own reasoning.
- **Empty sets.** Two empty sets score 0; this was the agent's own choice. One empty set scores the domain diagonal, Metrics Reloaded's worst value. Both are recorded in [issue 102](../../issues/102-shock-set-distance-empty-and-non-positive-conventions.md).
- **Non-positive pressure.** At first, a cell beside a non-positive pressure was flagged. That behaviour fell out of the multiplicative test. In the review round it was replaced by "never flagged". Three options were weighed:
  - Keeping the flag inverts the ranking, as measured.
  - Returning NaN on any non-positive value is the clean theory, but the contract tests feed a signed field and require a finite number.
  - Never flagging means a field that is not a thermodynamic pressure has no shock and scores like a missed one. A positivity failure away from the shock becomes invisible.

  The builder took the third option, as its own reasoning, and recorded it in issue 102. No judgement file was written, because issue 102 already held the convention.
- **Non-finite fields** score NaN, as every other registered metric already returns. This was the agent's own reasoning.
- **Pre-registration** (in the commit message of d9dcb84 on #22):
  - `translate_x`, direction +1: an integer translation moves both detected cells by the severity.
  - `translate_subpixel`, direction +1: the cells follow in whole-cell steps, with ties at the smallest shifts.
  - `additive_noise`, direction +1: noise whose cell-to-cell ratio reaches the Mach 1.3 ratio is flagged away from the true shock, more so as the amplitude grows.
  - No blind axis was declared, because no enabled operator preserves the shock position. The blur and spectral axes were left out, because both a kept and a lost shock are plausible there and neither is a controlled change of position.
  - The declared fields were pressure, with lower better, units of length and a mean reduction.

## Rejected formulations and negative results

- **The normalised pressure-gradient sensor of the deleted detector idea page.** It was rejected because no primary source fixes its threshold. The searches turned up only tunable thresholds, for example GP-MOOD (Bourgeois & Lee 2022, JCP, arXiv 2110.08683, Sect. 3.1, "heuristically tunable").
- **A two-point face stencil.** It would recover $|x - x'|$ exactly for a sharp step, but it sees even less of a captured shock spread over several cells.
- **Smoothing and spectral axes carry no distance.** On `sod_shock_tube`, every calibrated smoothing strength (`gaussian_blur`, `box_blur`) removes the shock from the three-cell detector. So does every lowpass strength in most frames, and `coarsen_bandlimited` from its second strength on. The metric then scores the empty-set diagonal. No local detector finds a shock smeared over many cells. That includes the full Schaal-Springel criterion (iii), whose jump is also taken between neighbouring cells. A distance under smoothing would need a detector built for wide shocks, which is proposed in [proposals/MTR-shock_set_distance-full-shock-zone-detector.md](../../proposals/MTR-shock_set_distance-full-shock-zone-detector.md). An earlier version of this record attributed a whole-zone jump test to Schaal and Springel. That was a misreading, corrected in the review round.
- **Flagging cells beside a non-positive pressure.** This was rejected in the review round because it inverts the ranking (see Review round and [issue 102](../../issues/102-shock-set-distance-empty-and-non-positive-conventions.md), item 3).

## Open questions and follow-ups

- **The empty-set and non-positive conventions.** Two empty sets score 0, one empty set scores the diagonal, and a cell beside a non-positive pressure is never flagged. These are open in [issue 102](../../issues/102-shock-set-distance-empty-and-non-positive-conventions.md), to be settled together with `shock_mask_overlap`'s both-empty convention ([issue 120](../../issues/120-shock-mask-overlap-both-empty-convention.md)). The rejected flagging convention is a general pitfall for any future detector built on ratios of density, temperature or pressure: such a detector must leave non-positive values unflagged or make the comparison undefined.
